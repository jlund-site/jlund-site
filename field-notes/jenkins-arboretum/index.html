A Place to
Be Still

A peaceful summer morning at Jenkins Arboretum & Gardens, and a reminder that sometimes nature is all the reset the soul needs.

There is something special about a summer morning before the world gets busy. The air feels different, the light is softer, and everything seems to move at its own pace. On a beautiful July morning, I found myself exploring Jenkins Arboretum & Gardens, a hidden gem tucked away in Devon, Pennsylvania. I wasn't looking for anything in particular. I just wanted to get outside, enjoy the morning, and see where the paths would take me.

What I discovered was a place that seemed to invite you to slow down. Winding paths led me through beautiful gardens, past towering trees and little pockets of nature that felt miles away from everyday life. Around almost every turn, there was another quiet spot to sit, reflect, or simply take in the surroundings. I loved that. These weren't places designed to keep you moving. They were places that gave you permission to stop. I found myself sitting for a while, watching the morning sunlight filter through the trees, listening to the birds, and appreciating how peaceful everything felt.

I've always believed that nature has a way of resetting the soul. Life moves quickly, and so much of our time is spent thinking about what's next. The next meeting, the next project, the next place we need to be. Even when we have a moment to ourselves, it's easy to fill it with something. But sitting there that morning reminded me how important it is to occasionally do absolutely nothing. To be present. To notice the little things. To let your mind wander without feeling the need to accomplish anything.

There is something incredibly grounding about being surrounded by nature. The trees aren't in a hurry. The flowers aren't competing for attention. Everything is simply existing, growing, and responding to the world around it. Maybe there's a lesson in that for all of us. We spend so much time trying to keep up with life that we sometimes forget to actually experience it. And often, the moments that restore us aren't extraordinary at all. They're the quiet ones we almost overlook.

What makes Jenkins Arboretum so special isn't just its natural beauty. It's the feeling you get while you're there. The sense that you've discovered somewhere a little removed from the noise of the world, even though you're right in the middle of a busy part of Pennsylvania. It's a place to walk, think, meditate, or sit on a bench and enjoy the morning sun. There's no pressure to do anything more than that.

I left that morning feeling peaceful, refreshed, and reminded that we don't always need to travel across the world to find something meaningful. Sometimes the places that stay with us are much closer to home. A quiet garden, a hidden bench, a little sunlight coming through the trees. Simple experiences that somehow give us exactly what we need.

Maybe that's the beauty of nature. It doesn't ask us to be anything, accomplish anything, or figure everything out. It just reminds us to be here, right now, and appreciate what's in front of us. And sometimes, that's all the reset our soul needs.
