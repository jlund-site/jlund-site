A Hazy Sunday
at Brandywine

The last match of the season, tucked away in the rolling hills of Pennsylvania.

There's something about a Sunday afternoon in the rolling hills of Pennsylvania that makes you want to take the long way home.

Tucked away among the trees and open countryside is a polo field that feels almost like a secret. No grand entrance. No spectacle announcing its arrival. Just an enormous stretch of green, a gathering of spectators, and the unmistakable smell of wet grass, horses, and worn leather.

It was the last match of the season at Brandywine.

Along the sidelines, the crowd looked much as you'd imagine. A little country-club polish, a few carefully chosen outfits, and people who clearly knew their way around a polo field. Cars and chairs lined the grass, and everyone settled into the easy rhythm of a hazy Sunday afternoon.

Then the horses began to move.

From a distance, polo has a certain elegance. Up close, it's something else entirely. Powerful horses changing direction in an instant. Riders leaning impossibly far from their saddles, swinging mallets while keeping control of animals moving at extraordinary speed. It's fast, physical, and far more demanding than its genteel reputation might suggest.

What stayed with me, though, wasn't just the match.

It was the world around it. The horses waiting beneath the trees. The trailers parked in the grass. The quiet exchanges between riders and the people looking after their animals. The little moments that remind you how much work goes into making something look effortless.

By the time the afternoon began to fade, I realized I'd discovered another one of those places that makes traveling—even close to home—worthwhile.

A hidden field. A beautiful old sport. The last match of the season.

Not a bad way to spend a Sunday.
